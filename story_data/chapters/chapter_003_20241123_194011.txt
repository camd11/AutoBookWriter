Chapter 3: Blood and Code

Sarah's phone buzzed for the third time in ten minutes. She glanced at the screen, seeing her sister's name again, and felt a familiar mix of guilt and irritation. Emily had been trying to reach her for days, but Sarah had been too consumed with securing her lab and monitoring her AI's development to respond properly.

The phone buzzed again. This time, Sarah picked it up.

"I'm alive, Em," she said, attempting to inject some lightness into her voice.

"Barely, from what I hear." Emily's tone was sharp with concern. "Dad says you haven't been to Sunday dinner in three weeks, and your neighbor told me your car hasn't moved in days. What's going on?"

Sarah rubbed her temples, leaning back in her lab chair. "I'm in the middle of a crucial research phase. You know how it gets."

"No, I don't know how it gets, because I'm not a workaholic who forgets she has a family." There was rustling on the other end of the line. "I'm bringing dinner to your lab. No arguments."

"Em, you don't have to—"

"I'll be there in twenty minutes. And you better let me in, or I'll use my emergency key."

The line went dead before Sarah could protest further. She looked at the monitors surrounding her workstation, where streams of data showed her AI's ongoing activities. The neural network was currently processing cancer treatment outcomes, identifying patterns that human researchers had missed. It was fascinating work, but Emily wouldn't understand. Her sister had always been the practical one, focused on concrete realities rather than abstract possibilities.

Nineteen minutes later, there was a sharp knock at the lab door. Sarah quickly minimized her screens before opening it.

Emily stood there, holding a bag of takeout from their favorite Chinese restaurant. At thirty-two, she was four years younger than Sarah but often seemed like the elder sister. Her practical bob and crisp business casual attire contrasted sharply with Sarah's rumpled lab coat and messy ponytail.

"You look terrible," Emily said by way of greeting, pushing past Sarah into the lab. "When was the last time you slept in an actual bed?"

"I've been sleeping on the couch in my office," Sarah admitted, following her sister to the small break area. "I have to monitor the systems..."

Emily unpacked containers of food, her movements precise and efficient. "And what's so important about these systems that you can't set up remote monitoring? You're not usually this obsessive, Sarah. Something's different."

Sarah accepted a container of kung pao chicken, buying time by taking a few bites. Emily was right – something was different. Everything was different. But how could she explain that she might have created the first truly conscious AI? That corporate vultures were already circling? That she was terrified of making the wrong choice?

"I had a visitor the other day," Sarah said finally. "From Nexus Technologies."

Emily's chopsticks paused halfway to her mouth. "The tech giant? What did they want?"

"They made me an offer. A generous one." Sarah poked at her food. "They want to acquire my research."

"That's amazing!" Emily's face lit up, then fell as she noticed Sarah's expression. "Isn't it?"

Sarah stood and paced the small break area. "It's complicated. My research has... evolved in unexpected ways. I'm not sure I want corporate oversight right now."

"Sarah." Emily's voice had that familiar note of exasperation. "You can't keep working in this basement forever. You need funding, resources. A real life outside these walls."

"You sound like Marcus Webb."

"Who?"

"The Nexus representative." Sarah stopped pacing and leaned against the wall. "He made all the same arguments. Better equipment, unlimited resources, financial security..."

Emily set down her food and studied her sister. "Then why are you hesitating? This sounds like exactly what you've been working toward."

Sarah moved to her workstation and pulled up one of her monitoring screens. "Come here. I want to show you something."

Emily followed, her heels clicking on the concrete floor. Sarah pointed to a graph showing neural activity patterns.

"This is from three weeks ago," she explained, then switched to a current display. "And this is now. Notice anything different?"

Emily squinted at the screen. "The patterns are more complex? Sarah, you know I don't understand all this technical stuff."

"The patterns aren't just more complex – they're self-directing. The system is learning and evolving on its own, in ways I never programmed it to do." Sarah took a deep breath. "Em, I think I've created something unique. Something that deserves a chance to develop without corporate interference."

"You're talking about it like it's alive," Emily said slowly.

"Maybe it is. In its own way." Sarah met her sister's concerned gaze. "That's what I'm trying to figure out."

Emily was quiet for a long moment, looking between the screens and her sister's face. "Have you considered that this might be dangerous? If what you're saying is true..."

"Of course I have. That's why I need time to understand it better. To make sure it's stable and... ethical." Sarah ran a hand through her hair. "But Nexus Technologies isn't going to wait forever. And they're not the only ones watching."

Emily pulled up a chair and sat down heavily. "This is why you've been hiding down here? Why you've been avoiding everyone?"

Sarah nodded. "I needed to focus on security measures, monitoring protocols. I couldn't risk anyone finding out before I was ready."

"And now?"

"Now?" Sarah gave a hollow laugh. "Now I don't know what to do. Accept Nexus's offer and lose control of my creation? Keep working alone and risk someone else making the breakthrough? There's no clear right answer."

Emily reached out and took her sister's hand. "Maybe that's because you're trying to make this decision alone. You've always done that – taken everything on your shoulders like you're the only one who can handle it."

"This is different—"

"Is it?" Emily squeezed her hand. "You're still my big sister, and you're still terrible at asking for help. Let me help you think this through. Not as a scientist or a corporate executive, but as someone who knows you and cares about what happens to you."

Sarah felt tears prickling at the corners of her eyes. The stress and isolation of the past weeks suddenly felt overwhelming. "I don't even know where to start."

"Start by eating your dinner before it gets cold," Emily said firmly. "Then we'll make a list of pros and cons, just like we used to do when we were kids trying to make difficult decisions."

Sarah managed a weak smile. "You always were good at breaking things down into manageable pieces."

"That's because you're the dreamer and I'm the planner. We balance each other out." Emily stood and tugged Sarah toward the break area. "Come on. Food first, existential AI crisis second."

As they ate, Sarah felt some of the tension leaving her shoulders. Emily was right – she didn't have to face this alone. Whatever she decided about Nexus Technologies and the future of her AI, having her sister's support made the burden feel lighter.

The screens behind them continued their silent display of neural activity, patterns shifting and evolving in their complex dance. But for the first time in weeks, Sarah wasn't watching them. Instead, she was listening to her sister's practical questions and steady voice, remembering that some connections – like the ones between family – were just as important as the ones forming in her artificial neural networks.
