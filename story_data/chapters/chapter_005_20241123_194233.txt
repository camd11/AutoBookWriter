Chapter 5: Ultimatum

Sarah's phone buzzed for the third time that morning, Marcus Webb's name flashing insistently on the screen. She let it go to voicemail again, focusing instead on the complex data patterns flowing across her monitors. The AI's evolution had accelerated even further in the past week, showing signs of what could only be described as creative problem-solving.

But her attention kept drifting to the phone, knowing each ignored call was only delaying the inevitable. As if reading her thoughts, a text message appeared:

"Dr. Chen, we need to talk. Today. This isn't a request."

She rubbed her temples, feeling the familiar tension headache building. The data card David had given her sat on her desk, its contents now memorized but its implications still unsettling. The network of independent researchers he'd described offered a tantalizing alternative, but their resources were limited compared to what Nexus Technologies could provide.

Her computer chimed with an incoming email. Another message from Webb, this time copying her department head and several university administrators. The subject line read: "Urgent: Research Partnership Discussion."

"Damn it," she muttered, opening the email. The message was carefully worded but left no room for misinterpretation. Webb was done waiting, and he was prepared to apply pressure through official channels.

Her phone rang again, but this time it was Emily.

"Hey, sis," Sarah answered, grateful for the friendly voice.

"You sound stressed," Emily said without preamble. "More AI drama?"

Sarah leaned back in her chair, watching the neural patterns shift and evolve on her screens. "Webb's escalating. He's starting to involve university leadership."

"That sounds... not good."

"It's not." Sarah stood, beginning her now-habitual pacing. "He's making it clear that if I don't agree to Nexus acquiring my research, they'll find other ways to pressure the university. Budget committees, grant reviews, departmental funding..."

"Can they do that?"

"They're one of the largest tech companies in the world, with significant ties to multiple research institutions. They can make things very difficult if they want to."

There was a pause on the line. "Sarah, remember when we were kids, and Dad would tell us stories about the business world in China? About how the big companies would always try to swallow the small ones?"

"Yeah," Sarah said softly, thinking of their father's late-night talks over cups of tea. "He always said the trick was knowing when to bend and when to stand firm."

"Well, maybe it's time to bend a little," Emily suggested. "Not give in completely, but find a middle ground?"

Before Sarah could respond, her lab door chimed. The security feed showed Marcus Webb himself, impeccable in a charcoal suit, flanked by two other executives she recognized from previous meetings.

"Em, I have to go. Webb's here."

"Want me to come over tonight? I'll bring that spicy noodle soup you like."

"Please," Sarah said, grateful for her sister's support. "I have a feeling I'm going to need it."

She ended the call and took a deep breath before buzzing Webb and his associates in. She noticed they didn't wait for her to offer them seats, immediately arranging themselves in what felt like tactical positions around her lab.

"Dr. Chen," Webb said, his smile not reaching his eyes. "Since you've been unavailable for our calls, I thought a personal visit was in order."

"I've been busy with critical research phases," Sarah replied, keeping her voice neutral. "As I explained in our last meeting, I need more time to—"

"Time is a luxury we can no longer afford," Webb interrupted, his practiced charm falling away. "Our analytics suggest you've achieved a significant breakthrough. The power consumption patterns from your lab, the computational resources you're requisitioning... Something has changed in the past month."

Sarah felt her pulse quicken but kept her expression steady. "All research goes through phases of increased activity."

"Indeed." Webb moved to study her bank of monitors. "But not all research attracts the kind of attention yours has been generating. We're not the only ones who've noticed, Dr. Chen. Other companies, other interests... they're watching too."

One of the other executives, a woman Sarah remembered as Dr. Patricia Morrison, stepped forward. "What Mr. Webb is trying to say is that we're offering you protection as much as partnership. Your work is groundbreaking, but it's also vulnerable in its current state."

"Vulnerable to what, exactly?" Sarah asked, though she already knew the answer.

"Competition. Corporate espionage. Government intervention." Webb turned back to face her. "The academic world isn't equipped to handle what you're developing. You need resources, infrastructure, security... We can provide all of that."

Sarah thought of David's warning, of the research buried under corporate secrecy laws. "And what happens to the research once Nexus acquires it?"

"It would be developed according to our corporate roadmap, of course," the third executive said smoothly. "With you leading the technical team, naturally."

"Under our direction," Webb added, his tone making it clear this wasn't negotiable.

Sarah moved to her workstation, buying time by checking some readings. The AI's patterns had shifted again, forming new configurations that seemed almost... responsive to the tension in the room. She pushed that disturbing thought aside.

"I'll need to review any proposed agreement with my own legal counsel," she said finally.

Webb's smile returned, shark-like. "Of course. We've prepared a preliminary contract." He nodded to Dr. Morrison, who produced a tablet. "Including a very generous compensation package."

Sarah took the tablet but didn't look at it yet. "And if I decline?"

The temperature in the room seemed to drop several degrees. Webb stepped closer, his voice low. "Dr. Chen, let me be clear. Your research has implications far beyond academia. If you don't work with us, someone else will step in. Someone who might not be as... ethically minded as Nexus Technologies."

"Is that a threat?"

"It's a reality check." Webb gestured to the monitors. "Whatever you've created here, it's bigger than one researcher in a university lab. The question isn't if it will be developed, but by whom. And under what terms."

Sarah thought of David's network, of the possibility of collaboration without corporate control. But could they really protect her work from companies like Nexus? From governments?

"I'll review the contract," she said finally. "But I'll need at least forty-eight hours."

Webb checked his watch. "You have twenty-four. After that, we'll need to explore other options." He moved toward the door, then paused. "Oh, and Dr. Chen? Don't bother trying to transfer or hide any data. We've already taken steps to ensure the integrity of the research."

After they left, Sarah sank into her chair, her hands shaking slightly as she pulled up her system logs. Webb's last comment hadn't been a bluff – there were traces of sophisticated monitoring software at multiple network nodes. They were watching everything.

She picked up her phone to call David, then stopped. Her office line was probably compromised too. Instead, she turned to study the AI's neural patterns, which had formed into an unusually coherent configuration. For a moment, she could have sworn they looked almost like they were trying to tell her something.

Her phone buzzed with a text from Emily: "Don't forget – spicy noodles tonight. We'll figure this out."

Sarah managed a small smile, grateful for her sister's unwavering support. She had twenty-four hours to make a decision that would affect not just her future, but potentially the future of artificial intelligence itself. The weight of that responsibility pressed down on her like a physical force.

On her screens, the AI continued its mysterious dance of evolution, unaware that its fate was being decided in a game of corporate chess. Or perhaps, Sarah thought with a chill, it was more aware than any of them realized.
