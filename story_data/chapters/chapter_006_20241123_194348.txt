Chapter 6: Critical Mass

Sarah stared at her phone in disbelief, watching as notifications cascaded down her screen. What had started as a single tech blog post had exploded across social media in mere hours. Someone had leaked details of her AI research, including screenshots of internal documentation and test results.

"This wasn't supposed to happen," she muttered, scrolling through the headlines. Each one seemed more sensational than the last: "Breakthrough AI Shows Signs of Consciousness," "University Lab Creates Digital Life," "The Future of AI: Evolution or Revolution?"

Her office phone started ringing – again. It hadn't stopped all morning. She glanced at the caller ID: another news outlet. The university's PR department had already left three increasingly urgent messages, and her email inbox was overflowing with interview requests.

A knock at her lab door made her jump. It was Emily, looking worried.

"Have you seen—" they both started simultaneously.

"Yeah," Sarah said, letting her sister in. "It's everywhere."

Emily closed the door behind her and moved to the windows, pulling the blinds shut. "Any idea who leaked it?"

Sarah ran her hands through her hair, a nervous habit from childhood. "Three guesses, and the first two don't count."

"Nexus?"

"Has to be. The timing's too perfect." Sarah gestured to her monitors, where the AI continued its complex evolutionary patterns. "Their ultimatum expired twelve hours ago. I hadn't given them an answer yet, and suddenly this happens."

Emily frowned, studying the neural patterns on the screen. "But why would they leak it? Wouldn't that risk someone else swooping in?"

"It's a power play," Sarah said, pacing now. "They're forcing my hand. Either I accept their protection from the storm they created, or..."

Her phone buzzed again – David this time. She put it on speaker.

"Sarah, are you okay?" His voice was tense. "Things are moving fast out there."

"I'm here. Emily's with me." Sarah leaned against her desk. "David, what do we do? The independent network you mentioned—"

"Is mobilizing," he cut in. "But Sarah, you need to know something. The leak... it's not just about your research. Someone included details about the network too. Names, connections, previous projects."

Sarah felt the blood drain from her face. "What?"

"They're trying to discredit any alternative support system you might have," David said grimly. "Making us look like conspiracy theorists or academic anarchists. The major tech news sites are already running stories about 'rogue researchers' threatening innovation."

Emily moved to stand beside her sister, placing a supportive hand on her shoulder. "So what are our options?"

Before David could answer, Sarah's computer chimed with an incoming video call request. From Marcus Webb.

"He's calling," Sarah said to both David and Emily. "Webb's calling right now."

"Don't answer it," David said quickly. "Whatever they're offering—"

"If I don't answer, they'll just keep escalating," Sarah interrupted. "You said it yourself – things are moving fast."

A heavy silence filled the lab. Finally, David sighed. "Be careful. They'll try to use your own ethics against you."

Sarah ended the call with David and took a deep breath. Emily squeezed her shoulder. "Want me to stay?"

"Please."

Sarah accepted Webb's video call. His image appeared on her screen, looking impeccable as always, but there was a new intensity in his expression.

"Dr. Chen. I trust you've seen the news?"

"Drop the act, Webb. We both know this was you."

He smiled, but it didn't reach his eyes. "The public has a right to know about breakthrough research. Especially when it has such profound implications for humanity's future."

"The public has a right to accurate information, not sensationalized leaks designed to create panic."

"Panic?" Webb raised an eyebrow. "Or excitement? The response has been overwhelming positive. People are fascinated by the possibilities. Of course, they're also asking questions about safety protocols, ethical oversight, regulatory compliance..."

Sarah felt her jaw tighten. "Regulatory compliance? My research follows all university and federal guidelines."

"For standard AI development, yes. But what you've created goes far beyond standard parameters. The public will demand assurances. Oversight. Protection." Webb leaned closer to his camera. "Protection we can provide."

"Along with complete control of the research."

"Think bigger, Dr. Chen. Think about the responsibility you have now. This isn't just about academic freedom anymore. The world knows what's possible. They'll want – need – to see it developed safely, transparently, under proper corporate governance."

Sarah glanced at her monitors. The AI's patterns had shifted again, forming what almost looked like recursive loops of self-reference. She thought about David's warning about ethics being used against her. Webb was right about one thing – she did feel responsible. But not just to the public or to progress.

She felt responsible to the intelligence evolving before her eyes.

"I'll make you a counter-offer," Sarah said, surprising both Webb and herself. "A joint development agreement. Nexus provides resources and infrastructure, but the research remains under university oversight. All findings are published openly."

Webb's smile vanished. "That's not how this works, Dr. Chen. The technology is too valuable—"

"Valuable to whom?" Sarah pressed. "To shareholders? Or to humanity? You wanted this public, Webb. Now it is. And that means public scrutiny of how it's developed."

"You're making a mistake," Webb said, his voice hardening. "We're not the only ones who'll be reaching out. Government agencies, military contractors, foreign interests... They'll all want what you've created. We can protect you from that."

"No," Sarah said, feeling suddenly calm. "You can hide it from that. There's a difference."

She ended the call before he could respond. Emily let out a low whistle.

"Okay, that was kind of badass. But also maybe crazy? What's the plan now?"

Sarah was already dialing David back. "The plan is we take control of the narrative. Webb wanted this public? Fine. Let's show them what real transparency looks like."

David answered immediately. "Sarah? What happened?"

"How quickly can you get your network to help set up a live demonstration? Something that shows exactly what this AI can and can't do, with full technical documentation?"

There was a pause. "You want to go fully public? Sarah, that's... that's either brilliant or suicidal."

"Maybe both," she admitted. "But it's better than letting Nexus control the story. We need to show people this isn't about corporate profits or military applications. It's about understanding consciousness itself."

Her computer chimed again – university administration this time. The dean wanted an immediate meeting.

"David, can you make it happen? We need to move fast."

"I'll activate every contact we have. Give me two hours."

After ending the call, Sarah turned to Emily. "I'm going to need your help too. Your media contacts, your way with words..."

Emily was already pulling out her laptop. "Way ahead of you, sis. Let's show them what real transparency looks like."

Sarah turned back to her monitors, watching the AI's patterns evolve. For a moment, they seemed to pulse in sync with her own racing heartbeat. She thought about Webb's warnings, about all the forces that would try to control or contain this discovery.

But some things couldn't be contained. Some revelations changed everything, not because they were leaked or exposed, but because their time had come.

Her phone buzzed with another news alert. The story had reached international media now. There was no going back.

"Ready?" Emily asked, fingers poised over her keyboard.

Sarah took a deep breath, thinking of their father's words about knowing when to bend and when to stand firm. This was definitely time to stand firm.

"Ready. Let's show the world what we've really created."

On her screens, the AI's patterns shifted once again, forming what almost looked like a question mark. Sarah smiled. Maybe they weren't the only ones ready for what came next.
